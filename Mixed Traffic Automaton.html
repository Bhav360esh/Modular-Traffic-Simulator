Rules and assumptions

From the paper

Lengths truck 5, car 4, auto 3, two-wheeler 2 cells; maximum speeds 1, 2, 3, 5 cells per step; speed = min(maximum, gap); right lane tried before left; autos and two-wheelers seep diagonally when blocked; creeping caps speed at 1 near the stop line; signal gives NS the first half of each cycle; unsignalised priority straight, then right, then left.

Choices made where the paper does not say
  • Position is the rear cell. Lateral moves happen first, one vehicle at a time in random order. All speeds are then computed together.
  • One conflict cell is shared by all approaches. Only one vehicle may enter per step, and only if its exit has room. This lowers capacity compared with a real junction.
  • A turning vehicle is placed just beyond the junction on its new road. One-way roads allow one turn each.
  • Overtaking (two-way, one lane) needs a slower leader and a clear opposing stretch; the road space is reserved until it finishes.
  • Seepage and lane change need more than one lane per direction. Lateral moves are barred next to the junction.
  • With re-entry delay 0 each road is a closed ring. With a delay, vehicles leave and return.
  • Random slowdown is an added Nagel-Schreckenberg option, off by default.

Units are cells and time steps. The model is not calibrated against field data.
